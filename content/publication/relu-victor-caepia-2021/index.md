abstract: Activation functions are used in neural networks as a tool to introduce
  non-linear transformations into the model and, thus, enhance its representation
  capabilities. They also determine the output range of the hidden layers and the
  final output.  Traditionally, artificial neural networks mainly used the sigmoid
  activation function as the depth of the network was limited. Nevertheless, this
  function tends to saturate the gradients when the number of hidden layers increases.
  For that reason, in the last years, most of the works published related to deep
  learning and convolutional networks use the Rectified Linear Unit (ReLU), given
  that it provides good convergence properties and speeds up the training process
  thanks to the simplicity of its derivative. However, this function has some known
  drawbacks that gave rise to new proposals of alternatives activation functions based
  on ReLU. In this work, we describe, analyse and compare different recently proposed
  alternatives to test whether these functions improve the performance of deep learning
  models regarding the standard ReLU.
